Lab: The Manager's AI Desk

ABB AI for Leaders · Day 1 · About 40 minutes · No coding, no VM. You only need Abby (or Copilot) in your browser.

Why this lab

In the morning poll, most of you said that emails, weekly status reports, Excel analysis and long documents take most of your week. In this lab you will hand three of these tasks to AI, check its work, and leave with prompts you can use from Monday.

In plain words: AI writes the first draft in one minute. You remain the editor and the decision-maker.

By the end of this lab you will be able to:

  1. Turn rough project notes into a clean weekly status report.
  2. Reply to a difficult customer email in the right tone.
  3. Summarise a long document for your manager, and catch the mistakes AI makes.
  4. Save your best prompts in a personal prompt library.
Data rule: In this lab, use only the sample data given on this page (Axion Controls is a made-up company). At work, paste ABB data only into tools that ABB has approved, such as Abby. Do not paste confidential data into public AI tools.

5 min0. The prompt formula

A good prompt is like a good brief to a new team member. It has five parts. Remember them as R-C-T-F-C.

PartWhat you writeExample
RoleWho should the AI act as?"You are a project manager at ABB."
ContextWhat is the situation? Paste the data."Here are my notes from this week…"
TaskWhat exactly must it do?"Write the weekly status report."
FormatHow should the answer look?"A table with Done, Next week, Risks."
CheckWhat should it be careful about?"Do not invent numbers. Mark anything unclear."
In plain words: vague prompt, vague answer. Clear brief, useful answer.

12 min1. Weekly status report from rough notes

You manage the Axion Controls – Plant Monitoring Upgrade project. It is Friday evening. Your notes from the week are messy. Your manager wants the status report by 6 pm.

1. Predict2. Run3. Improve4. Check5. Save

Step 1 – Predict

Before you run it: do you think the AI will spot the biggest risk in these notes? Write your guess in one line.

Step 2 – Run this prompt

Prompt 1A · copy into AbbyYou are a project manager at an industrial automation company. Below are my rough notes from this week on the "Plant Monitoring Upgrade" project for our customer Axion Controls. Write a weekly status report for my manager with these sections: 1. Overall status (Green / Amber / Red) with a one-line reason 2. Done this week 3. Plan for next week 4. Risks and issues (with an owner for each) 5. Help needed from management Keep it under 200 words. Use simple English. Do not invent any numbers or dates. If something is unclear, write "TO CONFIRM". My notes: - dashboard v2 demo done on tue, customer happy mostly, wants alarm colours changed - 3 of 8 site gateways installed (Pune, Nashik, Aurangabad). remaining 5 slipped – vendor shipment stuck in customs - QA found 14 bugs in data sync, 9 fixed, 2 are critical (data loss when network drops) - Ravi on leave next week, nobody else knows gateway config - customer asking for go-live 15 Dec, our plan said 20 Dec - budget ok so far, ~62% spent - security review not yet started, needed before go-live

Step 3 – Improve (pick one)

Prompt 1B · follow-upNow rewrite this as a 4-line message I can post in a Teams chat to the steering committee. Start with the overall status colour.
Prompt 1C · follow-upAct as a strict programme director. Read this report and tell me the 3 questions you would ask me in the review meeting.

Step 4 – Check

Reveal: what a good answer contains

Status Amber (or Red). Top risks: 5 gateways stuck in customs; 2 critical data-loss bugs; single-person dependency on Ravi; security review not started; customer wants 15 Dec but plan is 20 Dec. Help needed: decision on the go-live date and support to clear the customs delay. If the AI said "Green", your prompt needs a stronger Check line.

Step 5 – Decide

Would you send this report after a 2-minute edit? What did you have to fix?

8 min2. Reply to a difficult customer email

The customer has just seen the go-live date in your report. They are not happy.

Customer email (sample)From: Meera Joshi, Head of Operations, Axion Controls Subject: Go-live date – not acceptable Hi, We were promised the monitoring system before our December maintenance shutdown. Now I hear the gateways are delayed and there are data loss bugs. This is the second delay this year. I need a clear answer by Monday on whether 15 Dec is possible, otherwise I will have to escalate to your leadership. Meera
Prompt 2A · copy into AbbyYou are a senior project manager. Write a reply to the customer email below. Tone: calm, respectful, honest. Do not over-promise. Do not blame the vendor by name. Include: - Acknowledge her concern in one line - What we know today (5 of 8 gateways delayed in customs, 2 critical bugs being fixed) - What we are doing about it - A clear commitment: we will confirm whether 15 Dec is possible by Monday 5 pm - Offer a 30-minute call Keep it under 150 words. Customer email: [paste the email here]
Prompt 2B · try a different toneMake it shorter and warmer, suitable for a customer I have a good long-term relationship with. Keep the Monday 5 pm commitment.
Copilot (M365 licence) users: you can do the same directly in Outlook with "Draft with Copilot", and add the same instructions.

12 min3. Summarise a long document – and catch the mistakes

Your director forwarded a long release review note and asked: "Can we release or not?" You have 5 minutes.

Release review note (sample)RELEASE REVIEW – Plant Monitoring Suite v2.3 – Axion Controls 1. Scope: v2.3 adds alarm colour themes, a new trend chart, and offline buffering for site gateways. Offline buffering was requested by the customer after two data-loss incidents in Q3. 2. Testing: 412 test cases were executed. 398 passed, 11 failed, 3 were blocked because the test gateway was unavailable. Of the 11 failures, 2 are rated critical: (a) when network drops for more than 90 seconds, buffered data older than 60 seconds is lost; (b) after a gateway restart, the trend chart shows duplicate points. The remaining 9 failures are cosmetic. 3. Performance: dashboard load time improved from 4.1 s to 2.6 s. Memory use on the gateway rose by 18%, which is within the 25% limit. 4. Security: static code scan completed with no high findings. The penetration test is scheduled for 2 December and has not been done. 5. Documentation: user guide updated; operator training material pending. 6. Recommendation from QA lead: do not release to production until critical defect (a) is fixed, because it breaks the main purpose of this release. A controlled pilot at one site (Pune) is acceptable if the customer agrees in writing. 7. Customer expectation: go-live before the December maintenance shutdown.
Prompt 3A · copy into AbbyYou are an advisor to an engineering director who has 2 minutes. Summarise the release review note below in exactly 5 bullet points, then give a one-line recommendation: Release / Pilot only / Do not release. Quote the exact numbers from the note. Do not add any information that is not in the note. At the end, list anything the note says is still pending. Note: [paste the note here]

Now check the AI like a reviewer

AI summaries often sound right but drop or change one key fact. Tick only what is true in your AI answer:

Your AI scored 0 / 5

Prompt 3B · make the AI check itselfCheck your summary against the original note line by line. List any number, fact or recommendation that you changed, missed or added. Then give a corrected summary.
In plain words: never forward an AI summary on a go / no-go decision without checking the numbers. Asking the AI to check itself (Prompt 3B) catches most slips.

Bonus4. From report to slides

If you finish early, turn your status report into a short steering-committee deck.

Prompt 4A · copy into AbbyTurn the weekly status report above into a 4-slide steering committee deck. For each slide give: a title (max 8 words), 3 bullet points (max 12 words each), and a one-line speaker note. Slide 1: Overall status. Slide 2: Progress. Slide 3: Risks and decisions needed. Slide 4: Next steps.
